Structure map: L5 H1; L7, L9 intro paragraphs; L11 H2 "1. Decide: split or do" (4 bullets, L13-16); L18 H2 "2. Start the parts" (4 bullets, L20-23); L25 H2 "3. Heading levels are H1 then H2 throughout, no skipped levels, numbering 1-6 is sequential, and the unnumbered closing section is correctly set apart. No Blockers.

1. Major. L25-30 (section "3. End your turn"). The heading covers only the paragraph at L27. The two bullets at L29-30 ("Answering from below", "Passing a message on") are rules for a later turn, after a question, permission request or message has woken the session; they are not about ending a turn. A session that returns to the document when a permission request arrives has no heading to find it under: "End your turn" is the last place it would look, and step 4's heading names reports only. Either a heading of its own between 3 and 4 (e.g. "Between reports: questions, permissions, messages") or moving L29-30 into step 4 would make the hierarchy match the content.

2. Major. L27, L34, L48-51 (steps 3, 4 and the closing section). Failure handling is spread across the steps and the closing section with overlap. L27 lists "a failed turn or a silent end" as wake events; L34 handles the report status "failed" ("message the session to try again, start a replacement, or carry on without it"); L48 handles a failed turn with nearly the same remedy ("message the session with courier_send to carry on, to report, or to try again; or start a replacement"). L20's "When the parts do not all fit the tree's limits at once ... start the rest in a group of their own once the first group has reported" duplicates L49's spawn-refusal rule. L30's "courier_stop those parts" duplicates L51. L39's courier_cleanup and L51's "cleanup: true" split worktree removal across two sections without saying that L51's parameter belongs to courier_stop. A reader arriving on a notice cannot tell from the headings whether to go to step 4 or to the closing section, and the two spellings of "failed" (a status word at L34, failed="..." at L48) are the only cue. The closing section should own every "something went wrong" rule, with step 4 limited to the four statuses, or the closing section should be dropped into the steps; not both.

3. Major. L16. The fixed-form reply lines are set in plain prose with no delimiters: split: no, because …; considered: <the parts you weighed> or split: <n> parts: … (one phrase per part); the root has a third form, split: 1 part: <the task>, to keep my context. A reader cannot tell whether "or" is part of the literal line or the prose, whether "(one phrase per part)" is literal or a gloss, or whether ", to keep my context" belongs to the literal third form (it does, per ONE_PART_LINE in protocol.ts L11). This is the one line the session must reproduce verbatim. Backticks or quotation marks around each of the three forms would fix it without lengthening the text. (The brief at text.ts L60 has the same raw string but ends the sentence with it after a colon, which is marginally clearer.)

4. Major. L11-44 (numbered steps). The structure presents a linear 1-6 sequence for a procedure that loops: L20 (second group after the first reports) and L23 (a dependent part starts after a merge) send the reader from step 5 back to step 2, and step 3 recurs after every courier_send at L30 ("then end your turn again"). Nothing in the headings or an intro line says the cycle exists, so a session mid-task that has integrated one group has no structural cue that steps 2-5 repeat. One clause in the intro (L9) or at the head of step 2 naming the loop would suffice.

5. Major. L13-16, L20-23, L29-30, L43-44 (bullet form). The bullets are dense multi-rule paragraphs with no lead keyword: L15 holds four rules, L21 five, L30 five, L44 four. Only L29-30 ("Answering from below:", "Passing a message on:"), L34's status words and the closing section's bullets (L48-51) begin with a lookup key. A reader who returns for one rule must scan 80-100 words per bullet in steps 1, 2 and 6. L34 in particular packs four statuses into one bullet where four short bullets, keyed by status, would match the closing section's pattern. The closing section is the model for the rest.

6. Major. L15 vs text.ts L57-59. The skill's list of legitimate one-session stages has two entries ("a core you stage before the rest ... or a review of the parts once you have merged them"); the brief, which the same child session reads, lists three, adding "the fixing of what a review found once you have verified it", and adds the test for "the whole task" ("One session alone, with nothing of the task done before it by you or by 2 or more parts, and fewer than 2 parts after it"). L15 says "as your brief says", so the skill cites the brief while enumerating fewer cases than it. The two lists should match or the skill should defer wholly to the brief. (Order of the steps themselves agrees with the brief at text.ts L61-64 and with the root role line at L77-81: recall, decide, say which, start in one group, end turn, check and integrate, one combined report. No ordering disagreement found there.)

7. Nit. L18-23 vs text.ts L43-44. The brief's isolation rule places "commit first: its worktree starts from your current HEAD" before starting an isolated child. Step 2 of the skill has no such pre-step; the sequence jumps from writing the task (L21) to starting it. The run-time warning in spawnText (text.ts L250-253) arrives after the spawn. One clause on L20 next to "isolate: true" would close the gap in the step order.

8. Nit. L3 (description) vs body. The description promises "Starting the parts, waiting without polling, relaying questions and permissions, reading reports, merging and reporting upward" (steps 2-6) and omits step 1 (deciding, courier_recall), the courier_note rule at L44 and the whole closing section. It also scopes loading to "before you split", whereas the body's first step is the decision whether to split. The list order matches the body's order, which is good; the omissions mean the skill list understates what the body holds.

9. Nit. L5, L9 vs headings. The H1 and L9 ("This skill says how the tools behave") describe a tool-mechanics document, but every heading is a procedure step and no section is organised by tool. The headings are the right design for the reader; the title and L9 are the mismatch.

10. Nit. L37-39 (step 5) and L25-27 (step 3). Form is inconsistent across sections: 1, 2, 4, 6 and the closing section are bullets only; 3 is paragraph then bullets; 5 is a single paragraph. L39 contains two distinct rules (merge path; deliver path) that would be two bullets by the pattern of the other steps.

11. Nit. Parameter and tool naming. Tool names are consistently bare (courier_spawn, courier_send), which is fine. Parameters are not: isolate: true, native: true, cleanup: true are written as key: value; model and variant (L22) and group (L20 "the same group name") are bare nouns, so a reader cannot tell that "group name" is the group parameter; maxDepth, maxChildren, maxTotal (L49) are bare identifiers; failed="..." and ended="without-report" (L48) use attribute syntax; git merge courier/<session> (L39) uses an angle-bracket placeholder while L16 uses both <...> and …. Status words at L34 ("done: verify it.") open sentences in lowercase with no marking, so "done" reads as a word rather than a value. One convention (backticks for every parameter, status and literal) applied throughout would remove the ambiguity.

12. Nit. L13, L15, L43, L44. The same role is named four ways: "At the root", "the root, the session nobody started", "At the top", and by negation "If a session started you". Root-only rules are scattered across steps 1 and 6 under these different labels, which hinders lookup.

13. Nit. L21 "(step 4)". The cross-reference resolves to the right step, but the rule it points to at L35 is about the orchestrator's own checks and its own unrelayed permission prompts, while L21 is about writing the child's checks so one rule covers them. The pointer is approximate rather than wrong. The other cross-references, "(step 5)" at L21 and L23, point correctly to L39.

14. Nit. L27. The sentence lists seven wake events but the document gives no dispatch map from event to section: group reports and blocked go to step 4 (L34), message without status to L30, question and permission to L29, failed turn and silent end to L48. Since findings 1 and 2 already ask for a re-homing of those rules, a one-line pointer here ("see 4 and the last section") would make the implicit table explicit.

Summary: hierarchy and numbering are sound; the main structural defects are the mislabelled step 3, failure handling split across three places, the undelimited fixed-form reply line at L16, and a linear numbering for a procedure that loops. The brief and role line in text.ts agree with the skill's step order; the skill disagrees with the brief on the count of one-session stage types (L15) and lacks the brief's commit-first pre-step for isolated children.
